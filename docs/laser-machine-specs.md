## Small-detail capability and commercial priority

After consulting technical staff, the owner reports that the machine **can cut detailed small objects**. Their example was a circle of diameter “30” with internal cutout detail. The diameter's unit was not explicitly stated, so retain it as an illustrative report rather than a verified Ø30 mm production specification.

This supersedes the earlier rough lower-size comparison with shuriken/kunai-sized objects. That comparison must not be enforced as a machine minimum or an automatic custom-order rejection rule. Numerical feature limits for bridges, holes, cut spacing, lettering, and tolerances still depend on material, thickness, setup, and the actual drawing.

**Commercial direction: focus the premade catalog on larger artwork and substantial gifts.** The owner reports small products have low profit margins. Small items are technically possible but lower priority commercially; they can be considered if pricing, quantity, setup, finishing, and packing produce a worthwhile job. Larger products also need costing rather than an assumed margin guarantee.

“Carve out” here records the owner's description of internal detail. It does not establish a surface-engraving or marking capability; confirm the actual operation if a design requires it.
